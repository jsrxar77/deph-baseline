## Description — EN

```
Musica Universalis is a 6-minute generative ambient piece for meditation, focus and quiet moments — tuned to A = 432 Hz, with a binaural layer. Best with headphones.

Built from one Pythagorean idea: a single four-note motif (D-A-G-D — a unison, a fifth, a fourth, an octave) that every voice shares, over one slow harmony of fourths and fifths. A drone, a floating pad and a free-rhythm arp breathe together from the first second; midway through, the harmony lifts into a "flight" — a Shepard tone, the classic auditory illusion of a scale that seems to climb forever — before the piece settles back and fades to the drone alone. No drums, no fixed beat.

The picture is a small number of lights drifting through 3D space on slowly turning orbits, seen through water: every note drops a ripple, and where two ripples cross, the water catches the light.

WHAT YOU'LL HEAR
• Key: D dorian, tuned to A = 432 Hz (instead of the standard 440 Hz)
• One shared motif and one shared chord progression (Dm6 – Gsus4 – Csus4 – Dm6) run through every voice — drone, pad, a free-rhythm arp, a held bass tone, glass-bell lead and a rising Shepard tone
• A binaural layer around D3: the beat slows from 10 Hz in the opening to 4 Hz by the end. It only works with headphones; on speakers it is just a slow pulse
• Everything is synthesized — no samples
```
