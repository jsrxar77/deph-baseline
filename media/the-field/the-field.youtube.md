## Description — EN

```
The Field is a 6-minute generative ambient piece for meditation and quiet focus — tuned to A = 432 Hz, with a binaural layer. Best with headphones.

The field vibrates. Its vibrations become frequencies. The frequencies organise into geometry. And the geometry manifests the whole.

On screen, half a million grains of dust lie scattered on a dark stone plate. As the music plays, they jump with every note, then gather along the lines where the plate stays still — the sound figures first drawn by Ernst Chladni in 1787. Every note draws a different figure, computed from the frequency of that note. Then the grains leave the plate one by one and rise into the air, and like cells dividing, circle after circle, they build the Flower of Life. At 4:16 three voices of the music, moving at three different speeds, arrive at the same note at the same instant — and the flower is complete. Then everything falls back into the field.

WHAT YOU'LL HEAR AND SEE
• Key: A lydian, tuned to A = 432 Hz (instead of the standard 440 Hz)
• One seven-note motif shared by every voice, with a second voice that only plays the notes of A major (the "tintinnabuli" technique of Arvo Pärt)
• A proportional canon: the same motif at three speeds in Fibonacci proportion (21 : 13 : 8), timed so the three voices converge at 4:16
• An arpeggio made of the real harmonic series of each chord
• A binaural layer around A3 (216 Hz): the beat slows from 7 Hz to 4 Hz across the piece. It only works with headphones
• Sand figures computed from the equation of a vibrating square plate (Chladni figures), a new one for each note, chosen by its frequency — a simulation, not filmed sand
• No drums, no fixed beat. Everything is synthesized — no samples

CHAPTERS
0:00 The field vibrates
1:04 Frequency — the figures appear
2:56 Geometry — the Flower of Life is born
4:16 The whole
5:04 Return to the field

ABOUT THE MAKING
Written as code with Strudel (live-coding music) and rendered to audio; the picture is a particle simulation made with Remotion and Three.js, moved by the music's own events — built in collaboration with an AI assistant (Claude). Influences: Arvo Pärt's tintinnabuli, Steve Reich's chord cycles, Brian Eno's looping systems, and Ernst Chladni's sound figures.

This is music, not medical advice or treatment. Binaural layers work only with headphones.
```
